Gratuity calculation formula India

Gratuity, a significant component of retirement benefits, is a payment made to an employee by their employer at the time of retirement or resignation. The gratuity amount is based on the employee's last drawn salary and the length of their service. In India, the gratuity calculation formula is governed by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. **Understanding the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972** The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, applies to all establishments employing 10 or more workers and provides for the payment of gratuity to eligible employees at the time of their retirement or resignation. An establishment that employs less than 10 workers may also be covered under the Act if the Central Government so directs. **Eligibility criteria for gratuity** To be eligible for gratuity, an employee must have completed at least 5 years of service with the same employer. The employee must also be a citizen of India and not below the age of 60 years. **Gratuity calculation formula in India** The gratuity calculation formula in India is as follows: Gratuity = (15 days' salary x number of years of service) / 26 Where: * Salary is the last drawn salary of the employee * Number of years of service is the period for which the employee has rendered continuous service to the same employer * 15 days' salary is the average salary for each month, multiplied by 15 * 26 is the number of working days in a month Let's illustrate this with an example. Suppose an employee has a last drawn salary of ₹1,25,000 and has completed 20 years of service. The gratuity calculation would be as follows: Gratuity = (15 days' salary x 20 years) / 26 = (15 x 1,25,000 x 20) / 26 = ₹9,50,000 **Table: Gratuity calculation examples** | Last drawn salary (₹) | Number of years of service | Gratuity (₹) | | --- | --- | --- | | 60,000 | 10 | 2,87,000 | | 80,000 | 15 | 4,80,000 | | 1,00,000 | 20 | 9,50,000 | | 80,000 | 25 | 6,13,000 | As shown in the table above, the gratuity amount increases with the last drawn salary and number of years of service. **Important considerations** When calculating gratuity, it's essential to consider the following factors: * The employee's last drawn salary is taken into account, not the average salary. * The number of years of service is calculated from the date of joining the employer, not from the date of becoming eligible for gratuity. * If an employee resigns or retires before completing 5 years of service, they do not become eligible for gratuity. **Changes to the gratuity calculation formula** The Gratuity Calculation Formula has not changed since the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972. However, the Gratuity ceiling has changed in 1972, 2018, and 2021. The current Gratuity Ceiling amount in India is ₹20 lakhs. **Tax implications** Gratuity is taxable in India, and the employee is required to pay income tax on the gratuity amount received. The tax rate applicable to gratuity is 33.67% (30% + 10% cess). **Conclusion** In conclusion, the gratuity calculation formula in India is a complex process that involves considering various factors, including the employee's last drawn salary and number of years of service. By understanding the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, and the gratuity calculation formula, employees can calculate their entitlement to gratuity accurately. Employers must also be aware of the gratuity calculation formula to ensure that they make the correct payment to eligible employees.
