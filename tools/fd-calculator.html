Fixed Deposit in India — Complete 2026 Guide

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is the most popular guaranteed-return investment in India. It offers a fixed interest rate for a fixed tenure, with no market risk. Banks, NBFCs, and post offices all offer FDs at varying rates.

Tax on FD Interest

FD interest is taxable as "Income from Other Sources" at your slab rate. Banks deduct TDS at 10% if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens). If you are in the 30% bracket, you owe the additional 20%. Submit Form 15G/15H to avoid TDS if your total income is below the taxable limit.

FD vs Debt Mutual Funds

Both are taxed at slab rate now (post-Budget 2023 changes to debt MF taxation). FDs have: guaranteed returns, DICGC insurance up to ₹5L, and simplicity. Debt MFs offer: professional management, liquidity, and potentially higher returns. For amounts above ₹5L, debt MFs offer no insurance advantage — evaluate on expected returns.

Can I break my FD before maturity?
Yes. Premature withdrawal is allowed at most banks with a penalty of 0.5–1% on the interest rate. Some tax-saving FDs (5-year lock-in under Section 80C) cannot be broken prematurely. RBI has guidelines protecting depositors' right to premature withdrawal for all other FDs.
What is the 5-year tax saver FD?
A 5-year tax saver FD qualifies for Section 80C deduction (up to ₹1.5L per year) under the Old Tax Regime. It has a mandatory lock-in of 5 years. Interest earned is fully taxable. Under the New Regime, this deduction is not available — making it less attractive for most salaried employees from FY 2024-25 onwards.
