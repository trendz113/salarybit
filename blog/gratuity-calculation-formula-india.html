About Gratuity in India

Gratuity in India is a lump-sum payment to an employee by an employer at the time of retirement or resignation from service in certain cases. This is also known as separation gratuity. Gratuity payment is regulated by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, under which employers of all industries and establishments, employing 10 or more workers, are required to pay gratuity to eligible employees. The gratuity payment is tax-deductible and considered a part of the retirement benefits.

Eligibility criteria for Gratuity

Employees who have worked in an industry or establishment for at least five years are eligible to receive gratuity. This period is referred to as the 'continuous service'. The employee can also receive gratuity if he/her has completed 60 years of age with at least 5 years of service. Also, if an employee resigns or retires on medical grounds after completing five years of service, he/she is also eligible.

Method of calculating Gratuity

The gratuity amount is calculated using the following formula: {15 days' wages * number of years of service} = Total Gratuity Here, the term '15 days' wages' refers to: {30 days' basic salary * (12/365) * Total years of service} The gratuity amount is calculated as follows: {Total Gratuity} = Number of years of service * (Basic Salary * (12/365) * 15)

How to calculate Gratuity on a monthly salary?

The following example helps in understanding the Gratuity calculation: Suppose Mr. A has worked for 10 years in a company and is eligible for gratuity. His basic salary for 30 days is Rs 1,00,000. Then the Gratuity calculation will be as follows: Gratuity Calculation: 15 days' Wage = (30 days * Rs 1,00,000 * 12/365) = Rs 83,575 Gratuity = 15 days' Wage * Number of years of service = Rs 83,575 * 10 = Rs 9,35,750 So, Mr. A is eligible to receive Rs 9,35,750 as gratuity after completing 10 years of continuous service.

TDS on Gratuity and Important Points

1. TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is applicable on gratuity payments under section 192 of the Income-tax Act. 2. The employer will deduct TDS at the rate of 12 per cent if the employee's TDS certificate is available and 20 per cent otherwise. 3. The employer will pay the gratuity amount to the employee and deduct the applicable TDS amount. 4. The employee can claim a refund for the excess TDS deducted by submitting Form 26AS and Form 16 to the tax department. 5. If the employer doesn't have a PAN, the TDS will be at the higher rate of 20.6 per cent. 6. Payment of gratuity in excess of Rs 20 lakhs requires a TDS certificate from the employer.

Recent Changes in Gratuity Calculation

The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, provides a minimum amount of gratuity but does not specify the method of calculation. Employers can pay a higher gratuity amount, but it is generally based on the '15 days' wage formula. There have been several amendments to the act, with the most recent one being the 2007 Amendment. However, the core principles of gratuity calculation remain the same.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gratuity and how it is calculated?

Gratuity is a lump-sum payment to an employee by an employer at the time of retirement or resignation from service in certain cases. The calculation is based on 15 days' wages multiplied by the number of years of service.

Who is eligible for gratuity?

Only employees with continuous service of 5 years or more, or completing 60 years with 5 years of service, are eligible for gratuity.

Is TDS applicable on gratuity payment?

Yes, TDS is applicable on gratuity payments under section 192 of the Income-tax Act, and the employer will deduct TDS at the applicable rate.

Do we need to attach any documents with the gratuity payment?

Yes, you need to attach the necessary documents along with the Form 16 and Form 26AS to the tax department for claiming a refund.
