Gratuity calculation formula India

Gratuity is a benefit offered by employers to their employees as a form of appreciation for their service. In India, gratuity is regulated by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. The Act provides for a formula for calculating the gratuity amount, which is based on the length of service and the last drawn salary. In this article, we will discuss the gratuity calculation formula in India and provide examples to illustrate its application.

Eligibility for Gratuity

To be eligible for gratuity, an employee must have completed at least five years of continuous service with the employer. The Payment of Gratuity Act applies to all employers, including private and public sector companies, central and state governments, and local authorities.

Gratuity Calculation Formula

The gratuity calculation formula in India is based on the last drawn salary and the length of service. The formula is as follows: Gratuity = (15 days' wages for every year of service) × (total number of years of service) where the last drawn salary is taken into account for calculating 15 days' wages. To calculate 15 days' wages, follow the given steps: 1. Multiply the last drawn salary by the number of days of service and divide by 365. 2. Use the resulting amount as 15 days' wages.

Example 1: Calculating 15 Days' Wages

Suppose an employee has completed 10 years of service and the last drawn salary is ₹100,000 per month. The annual salary is ₹1,200,000. To calculate 15 days' wages, multiply the annual salary by 15 days and divide by 365. 15 days' wages = (₹1,200,000 × 15) / 365 15 days' wages = ₹52,054.08 Since the employee has completed 10 years of service, the total gratuity would be: Gratuity = (₹52,054.08 × 10) = ₹520,540.80

Example 2: Calculating Gratuity for Different Service Periods

To calculate gratuity for different service periods, follow the example below: | Service Period | Gratuity Amount | | --- | --- | | 5 years | ₹260,270 | | 10 years | ₹520,540.80 | | 15 years | ₹780,811.60 | | 20 years | ₹1,041,082.40 | As seen from the table, the gratuity amount increases with the length of service. The Payment of Gratuity Act recommends that the gratuity amount should not exceed ₹20 lakhs.

Taxation of Gratuity

The gratuity amount received is tax-free under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. However, if the gratuity exceeds ₹10 lakhs and the employee had received an advance on gratuity, a tax would be levied on the excess amount received.

Gratuity Payable to Widow/Surviving Spouse

In case the employee passes away before receiving gratuity, their widow or surviving spouse is entitled to receive the gratuity amount on their behalf. The gratuity payable to the widow/surviving spouse is calculated using the same formula as mentioned earlier.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the gratuity calculation formula in India is based on the last drawn salary and the length of service. The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, provides a formula for calculating the gratuity amount, which is as follows: Gratuity = (15 days' wages for every year of service) × (total number of years of service) By following the steps outlined in this article, employees and employers can calculate the gratuity amount payable to employees in India.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is gratuity taxed in India?

Gratuity received is tax-free under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. However, if the gratuity exceeds ₹10 lakhs and the employee had received an advance on gratuity, a tax would be levied on the excess amount received.

Is gratuity payable even if the employee leaves the job?

No, gratuity is payable only if the employee has completed at least five years of continuous service with the employer.

Can the employer claim tax deduction on gratuity amount paid?

Yes, the employer can claim tax deduction on the gratuity amount paid under Section 36(1)(ia) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
