Why Two Employers in One Year Causes a Tax Headache

When you switch jobs between April and March, you get two Form 16s — one from each employer. Each employer calculates TDS only on the salary they paid you, applying the full slab benefit and standard deduction as if that was your only income for the year. Neither employer knows what the other paid you unless you submit Form 12B to your new employer.

If you got a hike when you switched — common when moving jobs is a 20-40% salary bump — your combined annual income can push you into a higher tax slab than either employer individually accounted for. The result: you're under-deducted through the year, and the shortfall shows up as a demand when you file your ITR, sometimes with interest under Section 234B/234C for not paying advance tax on time.

Common Mistakes When You Have Two Form 16s

  • Standard deduction claimed twice — each employer applies ₹75,000 (New Regime) or ₹50,000 (Old Regime) independently, but you're only entitled to it once for the year
  • 80C or HRA claimed at both employers — if you declared the same investment proofs or rent receipts to both, deductions get double counted
  • Not paying self-assessment tax before filing — if there's a shortfall, this attracts interest the longer you wait
  • Assuming "TDS was deducted so I'm covered" — TDS from two employers rarely adds up to your true combined liability

What To Do If You Owe a Shortfall

  1. Pay the balance via Challan 280 on the income tax portal, under "Self Assessment Tax (300)"
  2. File before the ITR deadline (usually July 31) to avoid Section 234A late-filing interest on top
  3. Report income from both Form 16s under "Salary" in your ITR — don't file using only one
  4. Double-check you haven't claimed standard deduction, 80C, or HRA twice before submitting
Why didn't my new employer deduct enough TDS?
Unless you submit Form 12B (details of salary from your previous employer) to your new employer, they have no way of knowing your prior income. They calculate your TDS slab-wise assuming their salary is your only income for the full year — which usually under-deducts tax once both incomes are combined.
Can I still submit Form 12B after joining?
Ideally you submit it when joining. If you didn't, you can still manually combine both incomes yourself at ITR filing time and pay any shortfall as self-assessment tax before filing — you don't need your employer to fix it retroactively.
What if I'm owed a refund instead?
This can happen if your combined income is lower than what one employer assumed (e.g. you took unpaid leave, or moved to a lower-paying role). File your ITR with both Form 16s and the excess TDS will be refunded after processing.
