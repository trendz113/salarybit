How ESOP and RSU Tax Works in India — Complete Guide

ESOP (Employee Stock Option Plan) and RSU (Restricted Stock Unit) taxation in India involves two separate tax events: one at the time of vesting/exercise, and another at the time of sale. Understanding both is critical to accurate tax planning and ITR filing.

Stage 1: Tax at Vesting — Section 17(2) Perquisite

When RSUs vest or ESOPs are exercised, the difference between the Fair Market Value (FMV) and the exercise price is treated as a perquisite (salary income) under Section 17(2) of the Income Tax Act. This is taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate and is subject to TDS by your employer.

Perquisite Value = (FMV at Vesting − Exercise Price) × Number of Shares

Your employer should reflect this perquisite in your Form 16 and deduct TDS accordingly. If they don't (common with foreign company RSUs where TDS is often missed), you must declare it in ITR and pay self-assessment tax.

Stage 2: Capital Gains Tax at Sale

When you sell the shares, the gain from vesting price (which becomes your cost of acquisition) to the sale price is taxed as capital gains.

  • Indian listed equity: LTCG (12.5% after ₹1.25L exemption) if held >12 months; STCG (20%) if ≤12 months
  • US/Foreign equity: LTCG (12.5%) if held >24 months; STCG (slab rate) if ≤24 months

DTAA Relief for US RSUs

If you are a tax resident of India holding US company RSUs, and US taxes were withheld (W-2 or 1042-S), you can claim Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) to avoid double taxation under the India-USA Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA). You must file Form 67 before the ITR due date. The relief is calculated on a proportional basis and offsets Indian tax payable on the foreign income.

Frequently Asked Questions

My employer is a US company. Who deducts TDS on my RSU perquisite?
Your Indian subsidiary employer (the entity that pays your salary in India) is responsible for calculating and deducting TDS on the RSU perquisite under Section 192. Many companies get this wrong — check your Form 16 Part B for the perquisite entry. If missing, you must self-declare in ITR and pay tax plus interest.
What is the cost of acquisition for capital gains on RSUs?
The FMV at the time of vesting (the same value used to calculate the perquisite) becomes your cost of acquisition for capital gains purposes. This prevents double taxation on the same amount. Keep records of vesting dates and FMV from your broker/company statements.
I sold my RSUs immediately at vesting. Do I still have capital gains?
If you sell on the same day as vesting, theoretically the capital gain is zero (sale price ≈ FMV at vesting). However, due to currency conversion differences and intraday price movements, there may be a small STCG or loss. This is a common scenario for US RSUs sold same-day — the entire tax event is essentially the perquisite at vesting.
How to report US RSU income in Indian ITR?
Use ITR-2 or ITR-3. Report: (1) Perquisite income in Schedule S (Salary) — should match Form 16. (2) Capital gains in Schedule CG. (3) Foreign assets in Schedule FA (mandatory for all foreign equity holdings). (4) File Form 67 for DTAA relief if US taxes were withheld. Missing Schedule FA attracts penalty under the Black Money Act.
