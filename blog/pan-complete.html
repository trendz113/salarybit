I Spent 5 Years Trying to Fix a Duplicate PAN Card. Here's What Finally Worked.

Tax & Compliance 10 min read Updated 2026

I spent nearly 5 years visiting the Income Tax office, filing grievances, and waiting for calls that never came. I had two PAN cards on my name and no one would help me fix it — until I finally sat in the office and refused to leave. This is my real story, and a guide so you don't have to go through the same ordeal.

📖 My Real Experience

Like many people, I didn't even know I had two PAN cards until my name-change correction request was rejected by NSDL. Apparently, a second PAN existed in my name with a slightly different name format. That single rejection kicked off almost 5 years of frustration.

I filed grievances online. I visited the Income Tax office — not once, not twice, but over 10 times. Each time I was told to wait, come back, or contact NSDL. Emails went unanswered. The grievance portal kept showing "Fully Resolved" but nothing actually changed on the ground. NSDL still saw the duplicate as active and refused to process my correction.

Finally, I decided enough was enough. I took a day off work, went to the Income Tax office, and calmly but firmly explained: "It has been almost 5 years. I have had zero transactions on the second PAN. Why is this still not resolved?" That direct conversation — sitting there in person and not leaving — changed everything.

They asked me to surrender the physical duplicate PAN card along with a cancellation letter. But I had lost the physical card. So I filed an FIR (First Information Report) — which you can now do entirely from your mobile phone through your state's online complaint portal. I mentioned the card was lost and attached the FIR copy along with my cancellation letter.

One week later, I got a call: "Your duplicate PAN has been deleted. Come collect your documents."

But the story didn't end there. I then contacted NSDL to process my pending name-change correction — my primary PAN had my name as just "Deepak" while I wanted it corrected to "Deepak Kumar." Even after the deletion, NSDL initially hesitated. A few weeks later, I received the email confirmation with my corrected e-PAN attached — the one from NSDL/Protean eGov saying my request had been processed and the e-PAN was ready to download (password: your date of birth in DDMMYYYY format).

Total time: nearly 5 years. What finally worked: showing up in person, staying firm, and having the FIR copy ready.

Why Do Duplicate PAN Cards Happen?

Duplicate PAN cards are more common than you might think. They typically occur when someone applies for a new PAN while an old one (perhaps applied for years ago, possibly with a different name spelling) already exists in the Income Tax Department's database. Sometimes the duplication happens due to clerical errors, name mismatches, or when someone moves and applies again thinking they lost their original PAN.

The system may not always flag duplicates immediately, but it will catch up — usually when you try to make a correction request or during a KYC check by a bank or employer. And when it does, you enter a bureaucratic loop that can — as I experienced — last for years.

The Real Timeline: What 5 Years Looks Like

Here's an honest picture of how a duplicate PAN issue can drag on — and the key turning points:

Year 1 — Discovery

PAN correction/change request rejected by NSDL. Reason: a duplicate PAN exists in your name. Shock. Confusion. First visit to the Income Tax office.

Years 1–3 — Grievance Loop

Multiple grievances filed on the e-filing portal. Resolution status shows "Fully Resolved" — but NSDL still sees the duplicate as active. 10+ office visits. Emails sent to ward office. No real progress.

Year 4 — Escalation Attempt

Emails sent with CC to department helpdesk and NSDL. Grievance resolution letter obtained from the AO. NSDL still refuses to process the pending correction application.

Year 5 — The Breakthrough

Took a day off. Sat in the Income Tax office. Explained the situation firmly and calmly. They asked for the physical duplicate PAN card — which was lost. Filed an FIR online (via mobile) for the lost card. Submitted FIR copy + cancellation letter.

1 Week Later — Resolution

Received a phone call: "Your duplicate PAN is deleted. Come collect your documents." Followed up with NSDL. Name correction processed. e-PAN received via email from Protean eGov Technologies. ✅

⚠ Legal Warning Holding or using more than one PAN card is a violation of Section 139A of the Income Tax Act. It can attract a penalty of up to ₹10,000 under Section 272B. Even if the duplicate was issued by mistake, the responsibility to surrender it lies with you.

How to Know If You Have a Duplicate PAN

You may discover a duplicate PAN in several ways:

  • Your PAN correction or change request was rejected by NSDL, citing another PAN in your name
  • You received a letter from the Income Tax PAN Services Unit (NSDL) about the issue
  • A bank or financial institution flags it during KYC verification
  • You notice two different PAN numbers assigned to your name when checking the e-filing portal
"Your PAN is your financial identity. Having two of them doesn't give you more power — it creates legal risk."

Step-by-Step: How to Surrender the Duplicate PAN

Follow these steps carefully to resolve the issue legally and completely:

1
Identify Which PAN to Keep

Keep the PAN that has been used for filing returns, linked to your bank accounts, and Aadhaar. The other PAN — even if newer — should be surrendered. Most people prefer to retain their older, already-linked PAN.

2
Write a Cancellation/Surrender Letter to the Income Tax Department

Draft a letter addressed to your jurisdictional Assessing Officer (AO). State clearly your name, address, the PAN you wish to retain, and the duplicate PAN you wish to cancel. Mention that all past transactions have been done under the PAN you wish to keep.

3
File a Grievance on the e-Filing Portal

Log in to incometax.gov.in, go to the Grievance section, and submit a request for deletion of the duplicate PAN. Keep the acknowledgement number safe — you will need it for follow-ups with NSDL.

4
Don't Have the Duplicate PAN Card? File an FIR

If the physical duplicate PAN card is lost, you don't need to find it. File an online FIR (First Information Report) through your state's police complaint portal — you can do this entirely from your mobile phone. State that the PAN card was lost. Attach the FIR copy with your cancellation letter. This is an accepted substitute.

5
Submit Physical Documents to Your AO's Office — And Stay There

Submit your cancellation letter, copies of both PANs (or FIR if one is lost), identity proof, and affidavit. If you've been waiting months with no response, take a day off and sit in the office. Calmly explain the situation, mention exactly how long you've been waiting, and ask for a direct resolution. This personal, persistent approach is often what finally gets things moving.

6
Follow Up with NSDL

Once the AO marks the duplicate PAN as deleted in the backend, inform NSDL with your grievance acknowledgement number and request them to proceed with any pending PAN correction applications linked to your primary PAN.

💡 Hard-Won Tip (From Experience) Always follow up in writing — email creates a paper trail. If your grievance shows "Fully Resolved" but NSDL still sees the duplicate as active, attach the AO's resolution letter when writing to NSDL. Also: don't expect the system to connect the dots automatically. The Income Tax Department and NSDL operate somewhat independently — you often have to manually bridge the two with documentation.

Step 2: Correcting Your Name on Your Primary PAN

Once the duplicate is deleted, you can finally apply for a name correction on your primary PAN. In my case, my primary PAN had just "Deepak" while I wanted it to read "Deepak Kumar." This had to wait until the deletion was complete — attempting both together leads to rejection.

Here's how to apply for a name correction once the duplicate is resolved:

  • Visit onlineservices.nsdl.com (now Protean eGov) or www.utiitsl.com
  • Select "Changes or Corrections in existing PAN Data"
  • Fill in your correct name and upload supporting documents (Aadhaar, passport, voter ID)
  • Pay the applicable fee (approx. ₹110 for Indian address)
  • Note the acknowledgement number and track status online
📧 What to Expect When It's Done Once your correction is processed, you will receive an email from NSDL/Protean eGov Technologies with your updated e-PAN attached. The subject will say your PAN change/correction request has been processed. The e-PAN PDF is password-protected — the password is your date of birth in DDMMYYYY format. If you also requested a physical card, it will be mailed to your address separately. The e-PAN also contains an Enhanced QR Code readable via a specific Android app (search "Enhanced QR Code Reader for PAN Card" on Google Play Store).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just ignore the duplicate PAN if I never use it?
No. Even if you never actively use the duplicate PAN, the Income Tax Department's records still show it as active against your name. This can cause issues with loan applications, ITR filing, and any financial KYC. You must formally surrender it.
Will I be penalized if the duplicate PAN was issued by mistake?
The law does not distinguish between intentional and accidental duplication. However, if you proactively surrender the duplicate without having used it for any transactions, the chances of a penalty being imposed are minimal. Prompt action is your best protection.
How long does it take for the duplicate PAN to be deleted?
After filing a grievance, the AO typically resolves it within 30–60 working days. However, the backend update in NSDL's system may take additional time. Keep following up with both the AO and NSDL until the deletion is reflected in their records.
What if NSDL keeps rejecting my correction application even after the AO resolved the grievance?
Write a formal email to NSDL (tininfo@nsdl.co.in) attaching the AO's grievance resolution letter. Also CC the Income Tax Department's email for your ward. If the issue persists, file a fresh grievance on the e-filing portal with all documentary evidence.
Can I do all of this online without visiting the tax office?
You can try — and you should start online. But from personal experience: if months are passing and nothing is happening, showing up in person is often the only thing that actually breaks the deadlock. Take a day off, go to your ward's Income Tax office, calmly explain your situation and the exact duration you've been waiting. It works in a way that emails and portal grievances sometimes simply don't.
How do I file an FIR for a lost PAN card on my mobile?
Most states now offer online complaint portals through their police websites or apps (e.g., Karnataka Police — cybercrimesreporting.nic.in for lost documents). Select "Lost Property / Document", enter details about the lost PAN card (your PAN number, approximate date/place of loss), and submit. You'll receive an acknowledgement/FIR copy which you can download and print to submit to the Income Tax AO.
✅ Key Lessons From 5 Years of Experience
  • Do it in the right order: Delete duplicate PAN first → then apply for name correction
  • Lost the duplicate PAN card? File an FIR online from your phone — it is accepted as a substitute
  • Online grievances alone may not be enough — eventually visiting in person and being persistent is what works
  • Grievance status "Fully Resolved" ≠ NSDL updated — you must separately follow up with NSDL with the resolution letter
  • Once approved, expect an e-PAN email from Protean eGov with a PDF password-protected by your date of birth (DDMMYYYY)
