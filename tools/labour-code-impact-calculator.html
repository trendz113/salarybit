What Is the New Labour Code and Why It Matters

India's four Labour Codes — Code on Wages (2019), Industrial Relations Code, Social Security Code, and Occupational Safety Code (all 2020) — consolidate 29 older labour laws. The single biggest change for salaried employees is the Code on Wages rule that Basic Salary must be at least 50% of total gross wages (CTC). Most IT and private-sector employees today have Basic set at 30-40% of gross to keep take-home pay higher — this will change once states notify implementation.

What Changes for You

  • Basic Salary rises: If your Basic is currently below 50% of gross, your employer must restructure your salary to raise it — usually by reducing "special allowance" and adding it to Basic.
  • PF deduction increases: Employee PF is 12% of Basic. A higher Basic means a higher monthly PF deduction — reducing take-home pay in the short term.
  • Gratuity grows faster: Gratuity is calculated on Basic (Basic × 15 × years of service ÷ 26). A higher Basic means a bigger gratuity payout when you leave or retire.
  • Take-home may reduce initially: Higher PF and other Basic-linked deductions mean your monthly in-hand salary could drop even though your CTC stays the same.
  • ESIC applies below ₹21,000 gross: If your gross monthly salary is ₹21,000 or below, you and your employer both contribute to ESIC for medical and sickness benefits.

The Four Labour Codes at a Glance

  • Code on Wages, 2019: Basic wage must be ≥50% of total pay. Minimum wages now cover all workers, not just specific sectors. Overtime paid at 2× the normal rate.
  • Code on Social Security, 2020: PF and gratuity linked to the new higher Basic wage base. Gig and platform workers get social security coverage for the first time.
  • Code on Industrial Relations, 2020: Establishments with fewer than 300 workers can hire and lay off without prior government permission. Fixed-term employment is now standardised with the same benefits as permanent staff.
  • Code on Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions, 2020: Caps working hours at 48/week and 8/day, mandates annual health check-ups, and strengthens protections for migrant and contract workers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Has the New Labour Code actually been implemented yet?
The four codes have been passed by Parliament, but implementation depends on individual states notifying their rules. As of 2026, several states have notified rules while others are still pending — check your state's labour department for the current status where you're employed.
Will my take-home salary permanently reduce?
In the short term, yes — higher PF deduction on a higher Basic reduces monthly take-home. But this money isn't lost: it goes into your PF account (tax-free, compounding) and increases your gratuity entitlement, both of which benefit you long-term.
Can my employer reduce my CTC to avoid raising Basic?
No. Employers must restructure the existing CTC to comply with the 50% Basic rule — they cannot cut your overall CTC to work around it. If this happens, it can be raised with the Labour Commissioner.
Does this apply to all employees?
The Code on Wages applies broadly across establishments, but some provisions (like the Industrial Relations Code's hire/fire threshold) are specific to establishment size. Check with your HR team for how your specific employer is affected.
