Home Loan EMI in India — Complete Guide 2026

A home loan EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until the loan is fully repaid. It has two components: principal repayment and interest. In the early years, most of your EMI goes toward interest — this gradually reverses as the loan matures.

EMI Formula

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)

Where P = Principal, r = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = Total number of months

How to Reduce Your Home Loan EMI

  • Make part-prepayments: Even one extra EMI per year can cut your tenure by 3–4 years
  • Transfer to lower rate: A 0.5% rate reduction on a ₹50L loan saves ~₹3L in interest
  • Increase tenure: Lowers EMI but increases total interest — use as last resort
  • Negotiate at disbursement: Banks offer better rates to salaried employees with good CIBIL scores (750+)

Tax Benefits on Home Loan

  • Principal repayment (Old Regime): Up to ₹1.5L deduction under Section 80C
  • Interest paid (Old Regime): Up to ₹2L deduction under Section 24(b) for self-occupied property
  • New Regime: No deduction available on home loan interest or principal
What is the ideal EMI-to-income ratio?
Financial planners recommend keeping total EMIs (home + car + personal loans) under 40–50% of net take-home salary. For a home loan alone, aim for under 30–35% of take-home to maintain financial comfort.
Floating vs fixed rate — which is better in 2026?
Most home loans in India are floating rate (linked to RLLR/MCLR). With RBI rates stabilising post-2024 hike cycle, floating rates are generally preferred as they benefit from future rate cuts. Fixed rates suit those who prefer payment certainty and expect rates to rise.
Can I claim deduction on under-construction property?
Interest paid during the pre-construction period can be claimed in 5 equal instalments starting from the year of possession, under Section 24(b). The ₹2L cap applies to total deduction (pre-construction + current year interest). Only applicable in Old Regime.
